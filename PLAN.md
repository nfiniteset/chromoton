- **Depth-map cinema variation (Tier 0 of the 3D-source idea)** — new variation at `src/cinema/depth-map/`, labelled "Depth map" in the nav. Assigns target colors by _distance from camera_ rather than by source brightness. Deliberately **not** month-named: it's a technique experiment rather than a specific event's build, so it sits alongside the dated folders instead of claiming a month.

  Engine (additive, per [src/cinema/README.md](src/cinema/README.md)): [chromoton.js](chromoton.js) gained `setImageTargetsIndexed(imgData, palette)` — where `setImageTargets` thresholds luma into one of two colors, this quantizes luma into one band per palette entry, so a source can drive N target colors instead of 2. Plus `setImageDepthRange(far, near)` to spread the bands over only the slice of the source's range the subject actually occupies. `sampleImageIndices`/`applyIndexedImageTargets` mirror the existing mask/apply pair (same contain-fit downsample, same luma formula). Agitate carries over and applies uniformly across every band; auto-threshold and auto-dim explicitly don't — both are defined against a single white/black pair and have no meaning across N bands, so the panel drops them and `measureAutoDim` bails out in indexed mode. Existing variations are untouched: `setImageTargets` nulls the palette, so switching back is clean.

  Source: [src/cinema/depth-map/preprocess-depth.py](src/cinema/depth-map/preprocess-depth.py), a `uv` script (PEP 723 inline deps) running Depth Anything V2 **Small** (Apache-2.0 — Base/Large/Giant are CC-BY-NC-4.0) over the video via ffmpeg pipes, out to a grayscale depth mp4. Two temporal smoothers, both aimed squarely at the failure the motion-detection work hit: normalization bounds are EMA'd percentiles rather than per-frame min/max (this is what stops global brightness flicker), and the depth map itself gets a light EMA. Handles anamorphic sources — the placeholder stores 320x256 with a 64:45 sample aspect and displays 16:9, so output height comes from the _display_ aspect, not the stored one.

  Panel: "B/W Threshold" replaced by a **Depth** section (Bands 2-8, plus Far/Near range sliders, each held a step clear of the other) with a live swatch strip of the ramp actually in effect. Ramp is a plain far→near greyscale, 95% black to 95% white (the same two values the other variations use), defined as stops in `depthPalette.ts` and interpolated to whatever band count is set, so band count doesn't mean maintaining a palette per count — adding stops there is how you'd shape it non-linearly or bring colour back in.

  Verified: a synthetic luma-ramp test against the live engine puts band boundaries at exact quarters of the grid at full range, compresses them into the middle half when narrowed to 64-192, and confirms `setImageTargets` exits indexed mode; 2026-09 re-checked and behaves as documented. `DEFAULT_BANDS` set to 6 on the evidence of a band-mapping render of a real frame: at 4 bands the dancers fall into the same band as the corridor walls behind them, at 6 they separate from the floor. Next: retune the rest of `DEFAULT_*` (far/near, clarity, frame rate) against the finished depth render — the dancers sit in a fairly narrow mid-grey band with floor/ceiling blown to white, so the Far/Near sliders are where the remaining tuning lives.

- **Segments cinema variation (Tier 1 of the 3D-source idea)** — new variation at `src/cinema/segments/`, labelled "Segments". Colors cells by _whether a pixel is a dancer_: dancers 95% white, scene and background alike 95% black. Ladder context and model reasoning: [docs/3d-source-tiers.md](docs/3d-source-tiers.md).

  **No engine change was needed**, which is the notable part. Tier 0's `setImageTargetsIndexed` quantizes luma into evenly spaced bands, so [preprocess-segments.py](src/cinema/segments/preprocess-segments.py) writes each class at the luma sitting exactly at its band's _centre_; quantizing with `bands == class count` then recovers the class id exactly. At two classes that's luma 64/191 against a boundary of 127.5 — ±63 of headroom, so h264 can't corrupt it. Ordered signals (depth) and categorical ones (labels) share one code path; only the palette's meaning differs. The dependency is documented in `segmentPalette.ts`: palette length must match the preprocessing's class count, and the depth range is pinned to 0-255 in `CinemaApp.jsx` because narrowing it would shift the class boundary.

  Model is DETR ResNet-50 panoptic (Apache-2.0), which finds person instances confidently on this footage. Note this is _not_ the same as the ordinary two-color image mode: that thresholds the source's own luma, this decodes labels a segmentation model assigned — so a dark-clothed dancer against a bright floor still reads as a dancer.

  Started out coloring each dancer separately, which needed cross-frame identity DETR doesn't provide; greedy IoU tracking held within a shot but recolored the whole cast at every cut, since mask overlap can't survive an edit. Collapsing to one "dancer" class removed that failure mode outright rather than mitigating it, and also lifted the old 4-dancer cap — any number of detected people are simply white. The tracker is in git history if per-dancer color is ever wanted back.

  Panel shows a read-only **Segments** legend (Scene / Dancers) — nothing to tune, since the class count is fixed by the source. Throughput is the real constraint: DETR runs ~1.7fps on MPS (batch 4; batch 8 measured _slower_), so `--stride 3` keeps every 3rd frame for an 8.3fps output — costs nothing visible given the sim samples at <=10Hz and cinema plays at 0.25x. Full render is ~50 min.

  Full render done: 10,518 frames at 8.33fps, 1262s matching the source, 30MB. Mean dancer coverage 21.3% (up from 17.7% with the old 4-slot cap), only 6% of frames essentially empty of dancers. Encoding margin measured at native resolution across 42 frames: mean deviation from a class centre 0.04, 99.98% of pixels within +/-10, max 35 against a 63 boundary, zero pixels that would decode to the wrong class. Verified in-browser against the finished asset: only the two palette colors ever appear as targets.

- **2026-09 motion-detection video preprocessing** — batch-preprocess the source video before it hits the sim so a pixel changed at any point in the last N frames maps to white, unchanged maps to black, per `motion-detection-video-techniques.md`. Pure ffmpeg (`format=gray,gblur,tblend=all_mode=difference,lutyuv` threshold, then `tmix` over a window + re-threshold for multi-frame persistence) — no Python/OpenCV needed. Script: [src/cinema/2026-09/preprocess-motion.sh](src/cinema/2026-09/preprocess-motion.sh) (input/output/threshold/blur/window args; tuned threshold=48, blur sigma=1.5, window=5 against the forsythe placeholder — thicker, trailing silhouettes vs. single-frame diff, minimal background noise). Ran against the full placeholder video → `forsythe_one_flat_thing_reproduced_motion.mp4` (gitignored like all cinema media), wired into `CinemaApp.jsx`'s `VIDEO_SRC`. Verified the processed file itself is correct (thumbnail/extracted frames show strong, clean trailing motion silhouettes), but live in the sim with default settings it still reads as sparse/dark even after the 5-frame window made the source noticeably fuller — the generative engine's convergence dynamics (not the preprocessing) are the likely cause: this source flickers between two extremes (0/255) with a different spatial pattern most frames, unlike photographic video's smoothly-varying, spatially-coherent luma, so cells rarely get a stable target to converge toward. Needs tuning (frame rate, clarity/resolution, possibly the strategy in use) once a real video is picked — not chased further since it's a taste/tuning call, not a pipeline bug.

- **Dynamic contrast in cinema** — three independent toggles in the cinema control panel, each stackable, so they can be A/B'd live:
  - **Auto threshold** — retunes the black/white luma cutoff each frame so ~20% of the source image classifies as white. Disables the manual Threshold slider while on and live-updates it to the resolved cutoff instead.
  - **Auto dim white** — dims the white target based on how much of the grid the mask currently assigns to white (coverage, not convergence), gradated across [0%, 40%] → [full brightness, dimmed floor]. Stable/non-oscillating since the signal isn't affected by the dimming itself.
  - **Agitate target color** — a happy accident kept on purpose: the _original_ auto-dim measured actual population convergence, which turned out to be self-destabilizing (dimming knocks matched cells back out of range, which un-dims, which lets them match again, forever) and never let white settle — it stays "cloudy"/colorful, which looked great. Reimplemented as what it actually amounted to: a periodic small random nudge to the white target's brightness, no population measurement involved. First cut was a random _walk_ (each tick nudged off the previous tick's value), which could wander down and sit near the floor for a stretch — read as sustained mud rather than a flicker. Reworked to be non-cumulative: each tick independently picks dimmer/unchanged/brighter relative to the true base color, never off the prior tick, and the tick interval itself is jittered so it doesn't land on a mechanical beat.

  All three land in [chromoton.js](chromoton.js) (`setAutoThreshold`/`setAutoDim`/`setAgitateTarget`) and [CinemaControlPanel.jsx](src/cinema/2026-09/CinemaControlPanel.jsx). Next: keep A/B'ing against the footage, retune the auto-dim coverage range and agitate's step/interval to taste.

## Notes / Ideas

- **Perceptual color space (OKLab/OKLCH) as a deviance metric** — idea was to swap the sim's fitness calc (currently L1 distance in raw RGB) for Euclidean distance in OKLab, a perceptually-uniform space, plus a variant that rotates the target color's hue over time via OKLCH instead of the existing brightness-only agitate. Tried it (deviance-metric toggle + hue-rotate agitate, wired into both the main app and the 2026-09 cinema panel) and reverted — didn't add much visually to justify the extra surface area/complexity.

- **3D-derived intermediate video as the sim source** — instead of feeding the sim footage directly, analyse it (depth, segmentation, pose) and render an intermediate video whose channels carry _meaning_ rather than photographic luma, then drive target colors from that. Full write-up, including why it also fixes the convergence problem the motion-detection experiment hit, the per-tier stability requirements, and the licensing table that gates the whole ladder: **[docs/3d-source-tiers.md](docs/3d-source-tiers.md)**.

  Tier 0 (depth) and Tier 1 (per-pixel semantics) are built — see the two variations above. Tier 2 (posed 3D bodies → Blender render passes, or a live Three.js scene through `fromCanvas()`) is not started; its payoff is authorship (camera, retiming, recasting), not fidelity, and whether it's open at all depends on whether the event counts as commercial.
